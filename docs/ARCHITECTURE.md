## Event and deadline flow

Owner startup acquires and publishes Starting before any appearance delay, preventing duplicate bodies; control requests are handled during that delay. Startup validates host geometry and fit against the initial extent, chooses an alpha visual and maps the managed body with empty input SHAPE. The renderer publishes the initial frame. Startup verifies live placement and settles input obligations before restoring committed coverage with an input freshness boundary and publishing Ready. Startup observes the mapped body's layer without changing it. The loop processes bounded batches of up to 64 events, routing lifecycle/layout interruptions, fresh layer observations, popup events and body gestures; it uses one-event lookahead to avoid flushing a coalesced move across an interruption. Ordinary release flushes movement in completion order; cancellation discards pending movement first.

The CPU owner constructs an immutable request with `BodyPresentation::frame_request`, prepares it synchronously through `prepare_with` and hands the original request/candidate to `ProbeRuntime::publish_prepared_with`. That shared operation delegates successful timing to `FrameTiming::publish_with`. Deterministic asynchronous owner turns retain D1 as physical-work authority, take ready once after eligibility checks and finish checked publication in the same turn. Final `FrameGeometry::publish_with_admission` admission follows candidate/native-limit validation and precedes publication-state mutation and native submission; asynchronous tests recheck freshness there, rejecting equality. CPU admission adds no clock observation. `PreparedPublication::Skipped` preserves trustworthy committed pixels and leaves successful timing untouched: no completion-clock observation, committed sequence reservation or deadline rearming. Commitment permits completion timing; failure stops timing and retains the existing uncertainty/fencing rules.

Synthetic animation uses the existing event-loop deadline machinery and pauses during grabs, drags and menu interaction, resuming when eligible. For changed coverage, the runtime blocks body input and the renderer uploads to the backing pixmap before the checked empty input SHAPE and pointer release. Checked blit and flush precede installation of the exact candidate SHAPE and frame commit. The runtime installs the full enabling sequence after timing succeeds. Failure after submission invalidates usable authority and invokes safety fencing. A timing error after commit also explicitly revokes that authority and fences input.

No supervisor, worker socket, pidfd or Vulkan device is connected to this managed event flow. D3-B must separately integrate actual owner-observed drainage/fault/event/lifecycle/target/freshness predicates, retain the original admitted-job deadline, and consume request opportunities without spinning while physical work remains outstanding. Bounded work, no catch-up queue and successful-publication bookkeeping remain distinct requirements. [N01–N03](A01-C1-C-B2-D3-A-ACCEPTANCE.md#6-forward-d3-b-integration-obligations).

Stage D's visibility-transition foundations—checked empty input, pointer-release debt, gesture/menu/movement invalidation, dispatch eligibility and fresh visibility/placement predicates—are used for Hide and Show. Visibility requests cannot dispatch before settled pointer/popup obligations. Hide, including an already minimized no-op, requires the fence and fresh Iconic + Unmapped + HIDDEN confirmation. Local restoration requires Normal + Viewable + no HIDDEN, retained-monitor geometry and optional single confirmed correction before restoring the committed input region. An already-ready local Show is a harmless no-op; an off-workspace not-minimized body is not mapped or relocated. Minimized off-workspace Show removes HIDDEN and confirms coherent NotMinimizedElsewhere, possibly still Iconic + Unmapped, with unchanged body/current desktops and effective layer. Its qualified success has placement deferred and input unavailable; REMOVE-HIDDEN arms no post-map SHAPE synchronization. Later ordinary local return independently revalidates visibility, retained monitor, workarea and geometry/correction before restoring the committed input region, without resurrecting Show. Body-directed stale input is filtered against the enabling sequence; ordinary placement/lifecycle events cannot reopen the fence without validation.

Workspace/current-workspace drift fails as LayoutChanged; effective-layer drift fails as LayerChanged, without compensating writes. Show preserves effective Above/Normal/Below layer, not relative peer z-order within Normal. In owner H09, Openbox raised a locally restored mapped body relative to a covering peer through its own presentation behavior; DesktopRoomie sent no MapRaised, activation or explicit stack request. This is not Bring Top.

Root coordinates drive drag targets and geometry confirmation; local coordinates drive committed body-region and popup hits. Motion is coalesced before position requests. Body availability gates input: unmap/workspace interruption cancels interaction, remap requires live viewability/workspace, geometry and fit validation, and any one-shot bounds correction must be confirmed first. Destruction terminates the run and marks externally destroyed resources appropriately.

After a selecting popup's checked release and destruction, Above/Normal/Below dispatch uses fresh body/capability reads and at most one removal plus one addition. Already matching is a no-op; another selection while pending is busy. Later drag/popup ownership does not stall an existing layer operation. Relevant property notifications trigger fresh reads, not sequence-based layer confirmation. At the fixed one-second deadline there is a final fresh body observation, with no new mutation at expiry. Dismiss preserves pending layers; Quit, expiry and layout/body lifecycle abort them.

For Bring Top, visibility readiness immediately enters the same bounded layer controller; remote layer/visibility notifications cause fresh qualified observations while body ConfigureNotify remains available for ordinary movement. Local pending-layer servicing is excluded while the remote operation owns the controller. The loop checks deadlines even under sustained event traffic. The accepted M03.2 Stage-E correction services a new Show immediately after consumed control events in both routing sites and after the bounded batch before socket sleep; this fixed a real dual-monitor timeout where the owner waited until expiry before issuing MapWindow. M03.2 Stage F retains that scheduling for both Hide and Show, including off-workspace EWMH requests. Relevant visibility notifications trigger fresh observations; requests, events and phase changes never reset deadlines, and no new mutation starts at expiry. Buffered replies/events are drained before waiting and the connection is flushed; socket `poll` uses the earliest active deadline: Hide 1000 ms, Show 2500 ms (distinct from the 4 s caller budget). Bring Top uses 3500 ms owner / 2500 ms visibility / at most 1000 ms bounded layer, with 5000 ms caller; Hide/Show callers stay 4000 ms. Movement/correction confirmation uses 150 ms. Button-safety queries use 250 ms only during active button gestures (including chord suppression); ordered observations can cancel a missed release without inventing a click or final position. Idle has no permanent safety polling. Optional duration and layer deadlines share the same wait path.

Recover keeps one request, same-body identity and frozen destination through a one-way Here→Top handoff. Here must finish with fresh validated success before Top starts; the handoff clears all current success proof while retaining original baseline/layout, confirmed relocation/restoration history, possible dispatch and consumed attempts. Top reuses Show/geometry/input readiness and the sole Above controller, rebuilding current proof rather than inheriting Here's minimization/layer-preservation assertions. Recover-wide authority permits one correction only, with its full 150 ms confirmation interval; handoff or re-fencing cannot replenish a consumed or uncertain attempt. The 7000 ms owner total never changes: Here has at most 3500 ms, Top visibility has 2500 ms from actual validated handoff, and layer has at most 1000 ms after admission, all capped by the original total. The caller has 9000 ms from before discovery. Strict frozen destination/count/workarea/viewport/monitor/body-size evidence guards mutations, including between user-time and MapWindow and between layer removal/addition.

Recover Success requires fresh same-body membership on the frozen current workspace, not-minimized local visibility, exact Above, live geometry matching the committed extent and safe committed input coverage. Incomplete results are Partial only after a confirmed relocation and/or restoration transition; a no-op, correction or acknowledgement alone is insufficient. Here and both Recover phases share owner completion and the D4.1-derived fatal finalizer for service and external loop errors. A native failure invalidates current proof, preserves genuine history and original-error priority, permits only bounded read-only history observations, and safely re-fences input as needed. The single owner slot clears before at most one correlated reply attempt. Clean Quit/duration still result in Closing; actual selection loss remains OwnershipLost. No automatic retry, rollback or another owner is introduced.

Shutdown publishes Closing and settles control obligations with ordered control-endpoint cleanup. It cancels interactions and attempts pointer release before popup/renderer/body teardown and connection flush. Cleanup continues after failures; the original fatal error has priority over later cleanup errors. Explicit release records prevent fabricated success. Detailed rationale/tests remain in [M02.5](../experiments/desktop-probe/M02.5-IMPLEMENTATION.md), [M02.6](../experiments/desktop-probe/M02.6-IMPLEMENTATION.md) and [M03.1](../experiments/desktop-probe/M03.1-IMPLEMENTATION.md).
